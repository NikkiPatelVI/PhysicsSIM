Work

Work by a Constant Force
W = Fd cosθ
Work equals force times displacement times the cosine of the angle between them. Only the component of force parallel to displacement does work.
W — work (J) F — force magnitude (N) d — displacement magnitude (m) θ — angle between F and d
Where does this come from?

Work is the dot product of force and displacement vectors: W = F⃗ · d⃗. The dot product picks out the component of F along d (= F cosθ) and multiplies by the displacement magnitude. Only this parallel component transfers energy — the perpendicular component moves the object sideways, doing no work.

Work Signs
W > 0 (same dir)  ·  W < 0 (opposite)
Force and displacement in same direction → positive work (energy added). Opposite → negative work (energy removed). Perpendicular → zero work.
θ = 0° → W = Fd (max positive) θ = 90° → W = 0 (normal force, circular) θ = 180° → W = −Fd (friction, braking)
Where does this come from?

Directly from W = Fd cosθ. cos 0° = 1 (same direction → maximum energy transfer). cos 90° = 0 (perpendicular → no energy transfer, even if the force is large). cos 180° = −1 (opposing → energy is removed from the object). Normal force and centripetal force are always perpendicular to motion → always zero work.

Work by Friction
W_f = −f_k · d
Kinetic friction always opposes motion (θ = 180°), so work by friction is always negative — it removes mechanical energy from the system.
W_f — work done by friction (J, always ≤ 0) f_k = μ_k N — kinetic friction force (N) d — sliding distance (m)
Where does this come from?

Friction force direction is always opposite to velocity (θ = 180°), so W = f_k · d · cos 180° = −f_k · d. The energy isn't destroyed — it becomes thermal energy (heat) in the surfaces: ΔE_thermal = +f_k · d. Total energy is conserved; mechanical energy decreases by exactly this amount.

Kinetic & Potential Energy

Kinetic Energy
KE = ½mv²
Energy of motion. Always ≥ 0. Depends on speed squared — doubling speed quadruples KE. Mass and speed are the only variables.
KE — kinetic energy (J) m — mass (kg) v — speed (m/s)
Where does this come from?

Apply the work-energy theorem to an object accelerated from rest by a constant force F over distance d: W = F·d = ma·d. Using v² = 2ad → d = v²/(2a). So W = ma · v²/(2a) = ½mv². This work equals the energy gained — define that as KE = ½mv². The ½ and v² both come directly from kinematics.

Gravitational PE
PE_g = mgh
Gravitational potential energy relative to a chosen reference height (h = 0). Only changes in PE_g matter — the reference point cancels.
PE_g — gravitational PE (J) m — mass (kg) g = 9.8 m/s² h — height above reference (m)
Where does this come from?

Work done against gravity to lift mass m a height h at constant velocity: W = F·d = mg · h (force = mg upward, displacement = h upward, θ = 0°). This work is stored as potential energy: PE_g = mgh. Because only ΔPE matters in energy conservation, the reference height can be placed anywhere convenient — usually the lowest point in the problem.

Spring (Elastic) PE
PE_s = ½kx²
Energy stored in a compressed or stretched spring. Always ≥ 0 regardless of whether stretched or compressed (x is squared).
PE_s — spring potential energy (J) k — spring constant (N/m) x — displacement from equilibrium (m)
Where does this come from?

Work done compressing a spring by x: the force varies from 0 to kx (Hooke's law). The work is the area under the F-x graph — a triangle with base x and height kx: W = ½ · x · kx = ½kx². This is why PE_s is quadratic in x while the force is linear. Doubling compression quadruples stored energy.

Work-Energy Theorem & Conservation

Work-Energy Theorem
W_net = ΔKE
The net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy. This is the most powerful single-object energy equation on the AP exam.
W_net — total work by all forces (J) ΔKE = KE_f − KE_i (J)
Where does this come from?

From Newton's 2nd Law: ΣF = ma. Multiply both sides by displacement d: ΣF·d = m·a·d = W_net. From kinematics, a·d = (v² − v₀²)/2. So W_net = m(v² − v₀²)/2 = ½mv² − ½mv₀² = ΔKE. This works for any path, any forces — as long as you use the net work.

Mechanical Energy
E_mech = KE + PE
The total mechanical energy is the sum of kinetic and all potential energies. For gravity + spring systems: E = ½mv² + mgh + ½kx².
E_mech — mechanical energy (J) KE = ½mv² (J) PE = mgh or ½kx² (J)
Where does this come from?

Definition: mechanical energy is the energy associated with macroscopic motion and position. It's "useful" energy that can do work — unlike thermal energy (random molecular motion). When only conservative forces (gravity, springs) act, E_mech is constant. Non-conservative forces (friction) convert E_mech to thermal energy.

Conservation of Mechanical Energy
KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f
Holds when only conservative forces act (no friction, no air resistance). Total mechanical energy is constant throughout the motion.
Applies when: no friction, no air drag KE + PE = constant at every point
Where does this come from?

For conservative forces, the work done depends only on start and end positions, not path. Define PE as the negative of the work done by the conservative force: W_conservative = −ΔPE. From W-E theorem: W_net = ΔKE → −ΔPE = ΔKE → ΔKE + ΔPE = 0 → KE + PE = constant. This is why you can swap KE↔PE freely without worrying about how the object got there.

With Non-Conservative Forces
KE_i + PE_i + W_nc = KE_f + PE_f
When friction or other non-conservative forces act, they change the total mechanical energy. W_nc is negative for friction — it removes energy.
W_nc — work by non-conservative forces (J) W_nc = −f_k · d for kinetic friction ΔE_mech = W_nc (energy lost to heat)
Where does this come from?

The work-energy theorem always holds: W_total = ΔKE. Split work into conservative (W_c = −ΔPE) and non-conservative (W_nc) parts: W_c + W_nc = ΔKE → −ΔPE + W_nc = ΔKE → W_nc = ΔKE + ΔPE = ΔE_mech. For friction W_nc = −f_k·d, so E_mech decreases by f_k·d — that energy becomes thermal energy in the surfaces.

Power

Average Power
P = W / t
Rate of doing work or transferring energy. The same work done in half the time requires twice the power. Power is a scalar.
P — power (W = J/s) W — work done (J) t — time elapsed (s)
Where does this come from?

Definition: power is the rate of energy transfer. Just as velocity = Δx/Δt (rate of position change), power = W/Δt (rate of work). A 1 W motor delivers 1 J every second. A 100 W bulb consumes 100 J/s. Power describes how quickly energy moves, not how much total energy is involved.

Instantaneous Power
P = Fv
Power delivered by a constant force equals force times velocity (when F is parallel to v). Useful when speed, not displacement, is given.
P — power (W) F — force along direction of motion (N) v — speed (m/s)
Where does this come from?

Start from P = W/t = F·d/t = F·(d/t) = F·v. This is the instantaneous form when F and v are parallel. Example: a car engine delivering force F at speed v puts out power Fv. At constant speed F = drag force, so power = drag × speed. This is why highway driving (high v) uses more power than city driving even if F is similar.

Efficiency
e = W_out / W_in × 100%
Ratio of useful output energy to total input energy. Real machines are always less than 100% efficient — some energy is lost to friction and heat.
e — efficiency (%) W_out — useful work output (J) W_in — total work input (J)
Where does this come from?

Energy is always conserved (W_in = W_out + W_lost), but not all input energy ends up as useful output — some is converted to thermal energy by friction. Efficiency measures what fraction is "usefully" converted. A perfect machine (e = 100%) is impossible because some energy always goes to heat. On AP FRQs, efficiency problems typically ask you to find W_lost = W_in − W_out.

Energy Bar Charts (LOL Diagrams)

Reading a Bar Chart
Each bar represents one energy type (KE, PE_g, PE_s, E_thermal).
Heights represent relative magnitudes.
Frictionless: total bar height stays constant (KE + PE = same).
With friction: total decreases; a +E_thermal bar appears.
Common Conversions
Ball falling: PE_g → KE
Ball thrown up: KE → PE_g
Spring launching block: PE_s → KE
Block compressing spring: KE → PE_s
Friction: E_mech → E_thermal (heat)
