What AP Physics 1 Actually Tests

Exam focus Fluid dynamics questions are heavily conceptual — qualitative reasoning about pressure, flow, and buoyancy usually earns as many points as calculation. Don't just memorize formulas; know what each term means physically.

Big Idea 1 — Pressure increases with depth

P = P₀ + ρgh is linear in h. Two points at the same depth in a connected fluid are at the same pressure — even if the containers have different shapes. This is why a U-tube manometer works.

Big Idea 2 — Continuity means speed and area trade off

A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ is conservation of mass for incompressible flow. The fluid doesn't pile up anywhere. A narrower pipe → faster flow. A wider pipe → slower flow. Sketch the pipe and label what's bigger/smaller first.

Big Idea 3 — Bernoulli is energy conservation

P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant is just conservation of energy per unit volume. If speed goes up (kinetic energy density up), pressure must go down. This explains lift on a wing, flow in a Venturi meter, and why a shower curtain blows inward.

Big Idea 4 — Buoyancy depends on displaced volume, not object mass

F_b = ρ_fluid · V_displaced · g. The object's weight doesn't appear in the buoyant force formula — only the volume of fluid it displaces matters. A hollow steel ball and a solid plastic ball of the same outer volume experience the same buoyant force.

Common AP Exam Traps

Trap 1 Confusing absolute pressure with gauge pressure. Gauge pressure = P − P_atm. A tire gauge reads gauge pressure. Bernoulli's equation uses absolute pressure.
Trap 2 Applying Bernoulli where it doesn't apply. Bernoulli requires: steady flow, incompressible fluid, non-viscous fluid, along a single streamline. Turbulent flow, viscous fluids (honey), and compressible flow (gas at high speed) violate these.
Trap 3 Thinking a heavier object sinks faster. Objects sink or float based on density comparison, not mass alone. A massive cruise ship floats; a small pebble sinks. Compare ρ_obj vs ρ_fluid.
Trap 4 Forgetting that pressure is a scalar. It acts in all directions. Don't apply a direction to pressure itself — only to the force it produces (F = PA, perpendicular to a surface).
Trap 5 — Doubling pressure doesn't double exit speed In v = √(2P_gauge/ρ), exit speed scales as the square root of source pressure. Doubling P_gauge multiplies v by √2 ≈ 1.41, not 2. Likewise, quadrupling P_gauge is required to double exit speed. Anything under a square root scales sub-linearly — a common FRQ trap.
Trap 6 — Depth doesn't change buoyant force on a fully submerged object F_b = ρ_fluid · V_submerged · g depends only on the volume displaced, not on how deep the object sits. Lowering a fully submerged object deeper does not increase buoyancy because V_submerged is unchanged.
Trap 7 — Sealed-top pressure can be below atmospheric When a finger seals the top of a straw containing water, the trapped air can be at less than atmospheric pressure. At the bottom opening (exposed to air) P = P_atm; working upward with P_top = P_atm − ρgh gives P_top < P_atm. Don't assume any trapped gas must be ≥ atmospheric.

Problem-Solving Frameworks

Buoyancy Problems

  1. Draw a free-body diagram — weight (mg down) and buoyant force (F_b up). Add tension or normal force if needed.
  2. Identify V_displaced — if fully submerged, V_displaced = V_object. If floating, V_displaced < V_object and you'll need the floating condition.
  3. Apply F_b = ρ_fluid · V_displaced · g — use the fluid's density, not the object's.
  4. Apply Newton's 2nd law — ΣF = ma. For equilibrium (floating, hovering): ΣF = 0.

Bernoulli / Pipe Flow Problems

  1. Choose two points on the same streamline where you know most variables.
  2. Write the full Bernoulli equation between those two points before canceling terms.
  3. Cancel what's equal — same height? ρgh terms cancel. Same area (wide reservoir)? v ≈ 0 there.
  4. Use continuity first if you need to find a speed — A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ gives you the other speed.
  5. Check units — everything in Bernoulli has units of Pa (N/m²).

Must-Know Conceptual Scenarios

Hydraulic Lift

Small force on small piston → same pressure transmitted → large force on large piston. F₂ = F₁ · (A₂/A₁). Work is conserved: the small piston moves farther.

Venturi Effect

Fluid speeds up through a constriction (continuity) → pressure drops (Bernoulli). Used in carburetors, atomizers, and Venturi meters. The narrow section has lower pressure, not higher.

Torricelli's Theorem / Tank Drain

Fluid exits a hole at v = √(2gh). The exit speed equals that of an object in free fall from the same height. The hole's area doesn't affect exit speed (ignoring viscosity).

Apparent Weight in Fluid

W_apparent = W_actual − F_b = mg − ρ_f · V · g. This is what a scale reads when an object is submerged. A scale under the fluid reads more (reaction to buoyancy).

5-Point Exam Strategy

Free-response approach AP FRQs award partial credit generously. Write the correct formula first, substitute values second, solve third. A correct formula with an arithmetic mistake still earns most of the points.

What earns full marks

  • State which principle you're using (Bernoulli, Archimedes, Pascal, Continuity)
  • Show the general equation before substituting
  • Justify any simplifications (e.g., "the reservoir is large so v₁ ≈ 0")
  • Include units in your final answer
  • For qualitative questions: explain the direction of change AND the reason

Practice Problems

Try each problem on paper first, then expand the answer to check your work and reasoning.

Problem 1 — Sealed Straw

A 15 cm vertical straw is filled with water. You seal the top opening with your finger and lift the straw out of a glass — the water stays inside. What is the absolute pressure of the trapped air just below your finger? (P_atm = 101 325 Pa, ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³)

Show answer & explanation

Answer: P_top ≈ 99 855 Pa (about 1.5 kPa below atmospheric)

At the bottom of the straw the water is exposed to air, so P_bottom = P_atm = 101 325 Pa. Working upward through the water column to the trapped air at the top:

P_top = P_bottom − ρgh = 101 325 − (1000)(9.8)(0.15) ≈ 99 855 Pa

The trapped air is below atmospheric pressure — that's what holds the water up. Common trap: assuming any sealed gas must be at or above atmospheric.

Problem 2 — Floating Wooden Cube

A wooden cube of density 600 kg/m³ floats in fresh water (ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³). What fraction of the cube's volume is submerged below the waterline?

Show answer & explanation

Answer: 60% submerged

For a floating object in equilibrium, the buoyant force equals the weight:

ρ_obj · V_obj · g = ρ_fluid · V_sub · g

Cancelling g and rearranging:

V_sub / V_obj = ρ_obj / ρ_fluid = 600 / 1000 = 0.6

The submerged fraction always equals the density ratio. A denser object sits deeper; if ρ_obj > ρ_fluid, the object sinks completely.

Problem 3 — Pipe Constriction

Water flows through a horizontal pipe at 2.0 m/s where the cross-sectional area is A. The pipe narrows to A/4. (a) What is the speed in the narrow section? (b) Is the pressure higher, lower, or the same in the narrow section? Justify.

Show answer & explanation

(a) v₂ = 8.0 m/s

Apply continuity (conservation of mass for incompressible flow):

A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ → v₂ = (A / (A/4)) · 2.0 = 8.0 m/s

(b) Pressure is lower in the narrow section.

Apply Bernoulli at the same height (so ρgy cancels):

P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²

Since v₂ > v₁, the kinetic-energy term is larger at point 2, which means P₂ must be smaller than P₁. Faster flow → lower pressure.

Problem 4 — Submerged Cylinder on a String

A solid metal cylinder (density 8000 kg/m³, volume 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³) hangs from a string completely submerged in water. Find the tension in the string.

Show answer & explanation

Answer: T = 68.6 N

Free-body diagram: weight (mg ↓), tension (T ↑), buoyant force (F_b ↑).

Mass: m = ρ_obj · V = (8000)(0.001) = 8.0 kg → weight = 78.4 N.

Buoyant force (submerged in water):

F_b = ρ_water · V_sub · g = (1000)(0.001)(9.8) = 9.8 N

Equilibrium (ΣF = 0):

T = mg − F_b = 78.4 − 9.8 = 68.6 N

Tension is always less than the dry weight when an object is submerged — that's why things feel lighter underwater.

Problem 5 — Diver's Pressure

A swimmer descends to 5.0 m depth in a freshwater pool. Calculate (a) the gauge pressure and (b) the absolute pressure at that depth.

Show answer & explanation

(a) P_gauge = 49 000 Pa = 49 kPa

P_gauge = ρgh = (1000)(9.8)(5.0) = 49 000 Pa

(b) P_abs = 150 325 Pa ≈ 150 kPa

P_abs = P_atm + ρgh = 101 325 + 49 000 ≈ 150 kPa

At just 5 m of depth the absolute pressure is already ~1.5 atmospheres. Every 10 m of water adds about 1 atm of pressure — useful rule of thumb for diving problems.

FRQ Example — Annotated Scoring

How to read this Each green check shows a discrete scoring point the AP graders look for. The sample response below would earn full credit. Notice how each point is a separate sentence or step — graders give partial credit, so write distinct, labeled statements.

The Question (10 points total)

A pressurized water source delivers water through a horizontal nozzle at ground level. The water exits the nozzle into the open atmosphere and creates a vertical fountain. The gauge pressure at the source is P_gauge, and the source is wide compared to the nozzle.

(a) [3 pts] Derive an expression for the exit speed v of the water at the nozzle in terms of P_gauge, ρ, and g.

(b) [2 pts] Calculate v if P_gauge = 50 000 Pa and ρ = 1000 kg/m³.

(c) [2 pts] Find the maximum height the fountain reaches above the nozzle.

(d) [3 pts] By what factor must P_gauge change to double the fountain's maximum height? Justify your answer.

Part (a) — Sample Response 3 / 3

"Apply Bernoulli's equation between the source (point 1) and the nozzle exit (point 2), both at the same height (y₁ = y₂):"

P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²
Identifies pressures: P₁ = P_atm + P_gauge (source) and P₂ = P_atm (open nozzle).
Justifies v₁ ≈ 0 because the source is wide compared to the nozzle (continuity).
Solves algebraically to get the symbolic expression v = √(2P_gauge / ρ).

Part (b) — Sample Response 2 / 2

"Substitute into the result from part (a):"

v = √(2 · 50 000 / 1000) = √100 = 10 m/s
Correct substitution into the symbolic expression.
Numerical answer with correct units: v = 10 m/s.

Part (c) — Sample Response 2 / 2

"At maximum height the water is momentarily at rest. Use conservation of energy (or kinematics: v² = v₀² − 2gh with v = 0):"

h = v² / (2g) = (10)² / (2 · 9.8) ≈ 5.1 m
Identifies that v = 0 at maximum height and applies energy/kinematics correctly.
Numerical answer with units: h ≈ 5.1 m.

Part (d) — Sample Response 3 / 3

"From parts (a) and (c), combine the expressions:"

h = v² / (2g) = P_gauge / (ρg)

"So fountain height is linear in source pressure. To double h, P_gauge must also double."

Shows that h is proportional to P_gauge (combines results algebraically).
States the correct factor: P_gauge must increase by a factor of 2.
Provides a physics-based justification (linear relationship between height and pressure).
What loses points on this FRQ Common errors that cost a point each: skipping the v₁ ≈ 0 justification in (a), forgetting units in (b) or (c), or stating "P must increase by √2" in (d) — that confuses the relationship between v and P_gauge with the relationship between h and P_gauge.
