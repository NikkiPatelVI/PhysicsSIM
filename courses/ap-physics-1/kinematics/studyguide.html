What the AP Exam Tests

Exam focus Kinematics questions split evenly between calculation and conceptual reasoning. Expect motion graphs (x-t, v-t, a-t) in multiple-choice and a projectile or free-fall FRQ almost every year. Unit 1 is ~10–15% of the exam.

Big Idea 1 — Displacement ≠ Distance

Displacement is a vector (direction matters); distance is a scalar (total path length). An object that goes 5 m right then 5 m back has zero displacement but 10 m of distance. AP questions exploit this distinction constantly — watch for "how far from the starting point?" vs. "how far did it travel?"

Big Idea 2 — Acceleration ≠ Speeding Up

Acceleration means velocity is changing — that includes slowing down, changing direction, or both. An object with velocity and acceleration pointing opposite is decelerating. A car moving left that slows down has a rightward (positive) acceleration.

Big Idea 3 — Kinematic Equations Require Constant Acceleration

The four kinematic equations only apply when acceleration is constant over the interval. For free fall and projectile motion, a = −g is constant. If acceleration changes, the equations give wrong answers — use graphs or break the problem into constant-acceleration segments.

Big Idea 4 — Projectile Axes Are Independent

Horizontal and vertical motions don't affect each other. A ball dropped and a ball thrown horizontally from the same height hit the ground simultaneously — horizontal speed has zero effect on fall time. Time is the only variable shared between the two axes.

Reading Motion Graphs

Position-Time (x vs. t)

  • Slope = velocity (steep slope → fast; flat → at rest)
  • Horizontal line → object at rest (v = 0)
  • Straight line (non-zero slope) → constant velocity (a = 0)
  • Curve → changing velocity (acceleration present)
  • Object returns to start when x returns to its initial value

Velocity-Time (v vs. t)

  • Slope = acceleration
  • Area under curve = displacement (signed — below axis is negative)
  • Horizontal line → constant velocity (a = 0)
  • Crosses zero → object momentarily at rest, then reverses direction
  • Total distance = sum of absolute areas above and below the axis

Acceleration-Time (a vs. t)

  • Horizontal line → constant acceleration (kinematic equations apply)
  • Flat line at zero → constant velocity
  • Area under curve = Δv (change in velocity)

Common Exam Traps

Trap 1 — Sign errors Always define a positive direction at the start and write it down. If up is positive, then g = −9.8 m/s², downward velocity is negative, and downward displacement is negative. Mixing signs mid-problem is the #1 kinematics mistake.
Trap 2 — "At maximum height" ≠ total velocity = 0 For a projectile, v_y = 0 at the peak, but v_x is still nonzero. Total speed at the peak = v_x = v₀cosθ. Only a straight vertical throw has zero total velocity at the top.
Trap 3 — Picking the wrong kinematic equation Each equation is missing exactly one variable. List what you know and what you need before choosing. If you have v₀, a, and t and need Δx — use Δx = v₀t + ½at². Don't just grab the first equation you remember.
Trap 4 — Average velocity ≠ instantaneous velocity v_avg = Δx/Δt gives the average over an interval. Instantaneous velocity is the slope of the x-t graph at a single point. "What is the velocity at t = 3 s?" means instantaneous — read the v-t graph directly at that point.
Trap 5 — Horizontal acceleration in projectile problems There is zero horizontal acceleration once a projectile is airborne (no air resistance). a_x = 0 always. Only gravity acts, and it acts only vertically.

Problem-Solving Framework

1D Kinematics (straight-line motion)

  1. Define positive direction — write it down. Usually up or right is positive.
  2. List variables — write v₀, v, a, Δx, t and fill in what you know. Mark the unknown with "?".
  3. Choose the equation — pick the one containing your unknown and all your knowns (the equation missing the unused variable).
  4. Solve algebraically first, then substitute numbers with units.
  5. Check sign and reasonableness — does the direction make sense? Is the magnitude plausible?

Projectile Motion (2D)

  1. Decompose at launch: v_x = v₀cosθ (constant), v_y₀ = v₀sinθ.
  2. Horizontal: a_x = 0, so x = v_x · t. One equation, one unknown.
  3. Vertical: a_y = −g. Apply kinematic equations with v_y₀ and a = −9.8 m/s².
  4. Time connects the axes — solve for t using the axis where you have more information, then substitute into the other.
  5. Final speed: v = √(v_x² + v_y²). Direction: θ = arctan(|v_y|/v_x) below horizontal.

Must-Know Scenarios

Ball Thrown Upward from Ground

v₀ upward, a = −g. At peak: v = 0; use v² = v₀² − 2gh. Time to peak = v₀/g; total air time = 2v₀/g. Lands with the same speed it was launched (symmetric). If launched from a height, it's no longer symmetric — use Δx = v₀t + ½at² for total displacement including the height.

Projectile Off a Cliff (Horizontal Launch)

Launched horizontally (v_y₀ = 0) from height h. Vertical: h = ½gt² → t = √(2h/g). Horizontal range: x = v_x · t. Final speed: combine v_x (unchanged) and v_y = gt at impact using v = √(v_x² + v_y²).

Two Objects Meeting

Write a position equation for each object as a function of time: x₁(t) and x₂(t). Set them equal and solve for t. Works for chasing problems, objects thrown toward each other, or one dropped and one thrown upward simultaneously from different positions.

Finding Displacement from a v-t Graph

Displacement = area under the v-t curve. Triangle: ½ · base · height. Trapezoid: ½(b₁ + b₂) · h. Area below the t-axis counts as negative displacement. Total distance = sum of the absolute areas of all regions.

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Constant Acceleration

A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to 25 m/s in 10 s. (a) Find the acceleration. (b) Find the distance traveled.

Show solution

(a) a = Δv/Δt = (25 − 0)/10 = 2.5 m/s²

(b) Use Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 0 + ½(2.5)(100) = 125 m

Alternatively: Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t = ½(0 + 25)(10) = 125 m ✓

Problem 2 — Vertical Throw

A ball is thrown straight upward with an initial speed of 20 m/s from the ground. (a) Find the maximum height. (b) Find the total time in the air. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

Show solution

(a) At the peak, v = 0. Use v² = v₀² − 2gh: 0 = 400 − 2(9.8)h → h = 400/19.6 ≈ 20.4 m

(b) Time to peak: t_up = v₀/g = 20/9.8 ≈ 2.04 s. Symmetric flight, so total time = 2 × 2.04 ≈ 4.1 s

Problem 3 — Cliff Drop

A stone is dropped (v₀ = 0) from a 45 m cliff. (a) How long does it take to reach the ground? (b) What is its speed at impact? (g = 9.8 m/s²)

Show solution

(a) Use Δx = ½gt² (taking down as positive): 45 = ½(9.8)t² → t² = 9.18 → t ≈ 3.03 s

(b) v = gt = 9.8 × 3.03 ≈ 29.7 m/s

Check with v² = 2gΔx = 2(9.8)(45) = 882 → v = 29.7 m/s ✓

Problem 4 — Projectile Range

A projectile is launched from the ground at v₀ = 40 m/s at an angle of 30° above horizontal. Find the horizontal range. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

Show solution

Method 1 (range formula): R = v₀²sin(2θ)/g = (1600 × sin 60°)/9.8 = (1600 × 0.866)/9.8 ≈ 141 m

Method 2 (from components): v_x = 40cos30° = 34.6 m/s; v_y₀ = 40sin30° = 20 m/s

Time of flight = 2v_y₀/g = 40/9.8 ≈ 4.08 s. Range = v_x × t = 34.6 × 4.08 ≈ 141 m ✓

Problem 5 — v-t Graph Area

A v-t graph shows a car accelerating from rest (v = 0) to v = 12 m/s at t = 4 s, then decelerating back to v = 0 at t = 10 s. Find the total displacement.

Show solution

Displacement = area under the v-t graph.

Phase 1 (0–4 s): Triangle with base = 4 s, height = 12 m/s → area = ½(4)(12) = 24 m

Phase 2 (4–10 s): Triangle with base = 6 s, height = 12 m/s → area = ½(6)(12) = 36 m

Total displacement = 24 + 36 = 60 m

(Both areas are positive because v never goes negative — the car always moves in the same direction.)

Annotated FRQ Example

Question Setup

A ball is launched from ground level at v₀ = 30 m/s at an angle of 53° above the horizontal. Use g = 9.8 m/s² and sin 53° = 0.80, cos 53° = 0.60.

This is a 4-part, 8-point FRQ covering decomposition, max height, time of flight, and range — the canonical projectile question.

Part (a) — Find the horizontal and vertical components of the initial velocity. (2 pts)

1 pt

v_x = v₀cosθ = 30 × 0.60 = 18 m/s (horizontal, constant throughout flight)

1 pt

v_y₀ = v₀sinθ = 30 × 0.80 = 24 m/s (initial vertical component, upward)

Part (b) — Find the maximum height reached by the ball. (2 pts)

1 pt

Correct equation cited with v_y = 0 at peak: v_y² = v_y₀² − 2gh → 0 = (24)² − 2(9.8)h

1 pt

h = 576 / 19.6 ≈ 29.4 m

Part (c) — Find the total time the ball is in the air. (2 pts)

1 pt

Time to peak: t_up = v_y₀/g = 24/9.8 ≈ 2.45 s. By symmetry, total time = 2 × t_up.

1 pt

Total time = 2 × 2.45 ≈ 4.9 s

Part (d) — Find the horizontal range. (2 pts)

1 pt

Horizontal range equation stated: R = v_x × t_total (using constant horizontal velocity)

1 pt

R = 18 × 4.9 ≈ 88 m

5-Point FRQ Strategy

Free-response approach Kinematics FRQs often ask you to (1) find a quantity, (2) sketch or interpret a graph, and (3) justify a conceptual claim. Each part is scored independently — a wrong answer in part (a) doesn't block credit in parts (b) or (c).

What earns full marks

  • Write the general kinematic equation before substituting numbers
  • Show your list of knowns and unknowns for full method credit
  • For graph questions: label axes, mark key points (peak, zero-crossing), show correct shape (linear vs. curved)
  • Justify claims with physics: "because acceleration is constant, the v-t graph is a straight line"
  • Include units on every numerical answer
  • For projectile problems: explicitly state "horizontal and vertical motions are independent"
