Core Definitions

Displacement
Δx = x_f − x_i
Change in position — a vector. Direction matters. Not the same as distance traveled.
Δx — displacement (m) x_f — final position (m) x_i — initial position (m)
Average Velocity
v_avg = Δx / Δt
Displacement divided by time — a vector. Positive or negative depending on direction.
v_avg — average velocity (m/s) Δx — displacement (m) Δt — time interval (s)
Average Acceleration
a_avg = Δv / Δt
Rate of change of velocity — a vector. An object can accelerate while slowing down.
a_avg — average acceleration (m/s²) Δv — change in velocity (m/s) Δt — time interval (s)

The Four Kinematic Equations

Velocity — no Δx
v = v₀ + at
Use when displacement is not needed. Missing variable: Δx.
v — final velocity (m/s) v₀ — initial velocity (m/s) a — acceleration (m/s²) t — time (s)
Displacement — no v
Δx = v₀t + ½at²
Use when final velocity is unknown. Missing variable: v.
Δx — displacement (m) v₀ — initial velocity (m/s) a — acceleration (m/s²) t — time (s)
Velocity² — no t
v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
Use when time is not given or needed. Missing variable: t.
v — final velocity (m/s) v₀ — initial velocity (m/s) a — acceleration (m/s²) Δx — displacement (m)
Displacement — no a
Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t
Averages initial and final velocity. Use when acceleration is unknown. Missing variable: a.
Δx — displacement (m) v₀ — initial velocity (m/s) v — final velocity (m/s) t — time (s)

Free Fall

Free-Fall Acceleration
a = −g = −9.8 m/s²
Acceleration is always downward. Apply kinematic equations with a = −9.8 m/s² when up is positive.
g = 9.8 m/s² (gravitational acceleration) Sign depends on chosen positive direction
Max Height (thrown upward)
h = v₀² / (2g)
At maximum height v = 0. Derived from v² = v₀² − 2gh setting v = 0.
h — max height above launch (m) v₀ — initial upward speed (m/s) g = 9.8 m/s²
Time to Max Height
t_up = v₀ / g
Time up equals time down for symmetric trajectories. Total air time = 2v₀/g.
t_up — time to reach max height (s) v₀ — initial upward speed (m/s) g = 9.8 m/s²

Projectile Motion

Launch Components
v_x = v₀cosθ  ·  v_y₀ = v₀sinθ
Decompose launch velocity into independent horizontal and vertical components at the start.
v₀ — launch speed (m/s) θ — launch angle above horizontal v_x — horizontal component (constant)
Horizontal Motion
x = v_x · t  (a_x = 0)
No horizontal force → no horizontal acceleration. Horizontal velocity stays constant throughout the flight.
x — horizontal displacement (m) v_x = v₀cosθ (m/s, constant) t — time (s)
Vertical Motion
v_y = v_y₀ − gt
Vertical component is pure free fall. v_y = 0 at the peak; apply all four kinematic equations vertically.
v_y — vertical velocity (m/s) v_y₀ = v₀sinθ (initial vertical speed) g = 9.8 m/s²
Range (level ground)
R = v₀²sin(2θ) / g
Maximum range at θ = 45°. Complementary angles (e.g. 30° and 60°) give the same range.
R — horizontal range (m) v₀ — launch speed (m/s) θ — launch angle
Final Speed (any point)
v = √(v_x² + v_y²)
Combine components with the Pythagorean theorem. Direction: θ = arctan(v_y / v_x) below horizontal.
v — speed at any point (m/s) v_x — horizontal component (m/s) v_y — vertical component at that instant (m/s)

Key Values & Units

Constants
g = 9.8 m/s² (use 10 for quick estimates)
Free fall: a = −9.8 m/s² (up positive)
SI Units
Position / displacement: m
Velocity / speed: m/s
Acceleration: m/s²
Time: s
Scalars vs. Vectors
Vectors (have direction): displacement, velocity, acceleration
Scalars (magnitude only): distance, speed, time
